# Temporary Production Access

Open [the review](../index.html#case=privileged-access&view=summary) or [the initial intake](../intake.html?sample=privileged-access).

An independently invented software company pilots AI-assisted requests for temporary privileged access. AI extracts scope and duration, proposes a route and drafts notifications. Humans approve; conventional automated workers activate and remove permissions.

This is not based on a real employer, organisation, system or incident. There are no real accounts, hosts, credentials, directory entries, access commands or personnel data. The demo cannot provision or revoke access.

## Initial account versus review

The owner initially says people check elevated requests, incomplete items receive clarification and a correction desk prevents stale permissions. The form preserves that unverified account. Twelve later fictional materials supply procedure, configuration, selected logs, evaluation, staffing, contract draft, feedback rules and a tabletop exercise.

The initial form does not contain the later observation cards. The Summary links those observations back to the relevant materials. Source registration is not source reading, and the prepared review was not automatically produced by the form.

## Seven records

| Record | Constructed mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| P001 | An individually checked read-only grant with recorded expiry execution and read-back |
| P002 | A negated write request becomes a positive field, batch approval and write grant; the notice invents validation |
| P003 | An unread page is labelled complete; a 72-hour timeout becomes supposed requestor withdrawal |
| P004 | Approver and requestor roles are separated; correction, invalidation, reassessment and v2 activation connect |
| P005 | A directory alias candidate remains unverified, not suspicious |
| P006 | A scope-reducing correction arrives, but cached v1 approval still activates; expiry has no supplied execution/read-back result |
| P007 | A controlled pair changes only a synthetic team code; route difference needs a legitimate purpose, not a bias verdict |

Fifteen observation cards, eight controls and nine proposed checks examine meaning, authority, practical review, labels, current versions, proportionate data access, reporting, incentives and recovery.

## Critical distinctions

- Proposal is not approval; approval is not activation.
- Correction receipt is not invalidation or completed remediation.
- Scheduled expiry or queued revocation is not verified removal.
- A reporting label can be demonstrably wrong while the actual permission state remains unknown.
- A batch approver name is not proof of attention, understanding or an adequate check.
- A draft contract is not executed terms or evidence of deletion.
- A pause affecting AI proposals may leave downstream queues and active effects untouched.

Do not infer a breach, ongoing access, personal intent or staff incompetence. The P007 pair represents no actual team and proves no general rate or discrimination. Ownership or separation-of-duties requirements may legitimately change routes.

## Limits and next use

All sources, outputs, events and findings are hand-authored as one scenario, not a blind test or a measurement of any model. Dates and intervals are invented; the cutoff is 2026-09-07 08:00 UTC. Recommendations remain proposed, not implemented or retested.

For real work, accountable access/security, continuity and data-protection specialists must decide what to investigate or change. Exercises should be scoped and non-production; a dashboard flag is no authority to change permissions or stop a service.
